Target
One constant target for every data source on the symbol.
Pick the setpoint and the value it is measured against by name. Samples are paired by timestamp using the last setpoint written at or before each value, so the two tags do not have to share timestamps. Removing a picked source sets it back to automatic.
Percentage deviation from a zero or negative target is undefined. A source whose target reaches zero is shown in engineering units instead, and the footer says so.
Tolerance
Units follow the deviation setting above — percent of target, or engineering units. Both asymmetric limits are entered as magnitudes; leave one blank to reuse the symmetric tolerance.
Chart
Deviation around a zero baseline with the tolerance band shaded and excursions coloured.
One bar per sample from the zero baseline. Reads better than a line for sparse or intermittent data.
Process value and setpoint as two conventional traces with the gap between them shaded — for when the absolute values matter too.
Beyond a handful of traces the excursions overlap into one colour. Extra sources are dropped and counted in the footer.
Colour
High and low are deliberately different colours: running hot and running cold are different problems, and nobody should have to read the axis to tell them apart. With more than one source the in-tolerance colour gives way to the palette so the traces stay distinguishable.
Statistics
Time in band, mean deviation, worst excursion, excursion count and longest excursion. All durations are measured from the sample intervals, not the sample count, because PI stores on compression and samples cluster where the signal is busy. Below the warning percentage the panel turns amber; below 80% it turns red.
Axes
Equal extent above and below puts zero dead centre — usually what you want, because an asymmetric axis makes a small offset look like a trend.
