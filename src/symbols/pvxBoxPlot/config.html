Layout
Horizontal reads better with long tag names — they stay upright instead of being rotated.
Horizontal orientation only.
Sorting by spread puts the most variable asset at the front, which is usually the one worth looking at. Display order is the default so the layout stays predictable as data changes.
Boxes
By hour, shift, day or week, one data source is cut into consecutive periods in local time, oldest on the left. Use it to see whether a tag is getting noisier, or whether one shift runs it differently.
Each box needs samples: raise the resolution under Statistics for many short periods. When there are more periods than the maximum number of boxes, the latest are shown.
Overlay line
A dashed line for a setpoint or limit. A data source wins over a fixed value and is not drawn as a box. With boxes by time the line steps with the source's mean in each period, so a setpoint change shows where it happened.
Statistics
Tukey stops the whisker at the last value inside Q1/Q3 ± k × IQR and marks everything beyond it as an outlier. Min-to-max never produces outliers.
A diamond at the mean. Far from the median means a skewed distribution.
Pinches the box by ±1.58 × IQR / √n around the median. When two boxes' notches do not overlap, their medians differ meaningfully rather than by chance.
A dashed line at the median of all medians — useful for spotting a whole group that has shifted.
Outliers
Beyond a couple of hundred markers the outliers overplot into a solid bar. Extras are dropped from the drawing but the true count stays in the tooltip.
Scale
Off: every box shares one engineering-unit axis, which is the honest comparison but collapses when the tags have very different magnitudes (610 mA next to 0.4 µS/cm). On: each box is scaled to its own observed range, so the boxes stay comparable by SHAPE — spread, skew, whisker asymmetry — while the axis becomes percent and the real min/max move into each tooltip. It changes what the chart means, so leave it off unless the tags really are of different kinds.
Leave blank to fit the data. Ignored while normalising, which always spans 0-100%.
