Gases
Add methane (CH4), ethylene (C2H4) and acetylene (C2H2) in ppm. A source whose name says which gas it is is used as that gas in any order; otherwise the first three are taken as CH4, C2H4 and C2H2, and the footer says so. Further sources are ignored.
IEC 60599 applies the triangle only once the gases are above the levels typical for the unit: the shares of three tiny readings are noise. Below this total of the three, the point is still drawn but the zone is not named. Blank always names it.
Each point uses every gas's newest reading at that moment. A limit here leaves out a point where one gas's reading is older than this, as with a lab result entered weeks after the others; the footer counts them. 0 is no limit.
Triangle
The trail is the newest points in the display's time range, fading with age, so the direction a unit is moving shows. 0 draws every point in range.
Only the zones listed change: PD, T1, T2, T3, D1, D2 and DT. The zones are those of Duval's Triangle 1 in IEC 60599: PD partial discharges, T1 to T3 thermal faults below 300 °C, 300 to 700 °C and above 700 °C, D1 and D2 discharges of low and high energy, DT a mix of electrical and thermal.
