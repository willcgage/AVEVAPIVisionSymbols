Data
The distribution is drawn from one data source. Add more only if you want to overlay them for comparison.
Overlaid sources are drawn as translucent outlines on shared bins, up to four. Statistics and capability always describe the data source selected above.
A distribution needs samples to be stable. Below about 200 the bin heights move noticeably every time the display refreshes.
Bins
auto uses the Freedman–Diaconis rule, which is driven by the interquartile range and so is not thrown off by the occasional spike PI data is full of.
Time held weighs each value by how long it lasted, so a burst of samples during an upset does not outweigh hours of steady running. The axis reads in hours (or percent of time). The statistics, Cp and Cpk stay per sample.
A fixed width in engineering units. Overrides the bin count — use it when two displays have to be compared bin for bin.
Blank bins over the full data extent. A narrower range clips the chart only; the statistics and capability below still use every sample.
Specification limits
These are what turn a histogram into an answer. Without them the symbol shows what the tag did; with them it shows whether the process is centred and capable.
Either limit may be left blank for a one-sided specification; Cpk is then computed from the single limit and Cp is not defined.
Only bins lying entirely beyond a limit are recoloured — a bin straddling the limit still holds conforming samples.
Capability
Cpk under this value is amber, under 1.00 red. 1.33 is the usual industrial floor (four sigma between the mean and the nearer limit).
A PI time series has no rational subgroups, so sigma is the overall sample standard deviation — which makes Cp equal Pp and Cpk equal Ppk. The panel reports that honestly rather than inventing subgroups.
Chart
Horizontal puts the value on the vertical axis, so the symbol lines up beside a trend of the same tag.
Fraction of the bin width. Use 0 for a classic touching histogram.
Overlays
Scaled to the bars, so a distribution that hugs the curve is normal and one that does not is telling you something — two peaks, a hard clamp, a skew.
A mean well away from the median is the quickest sign the distribution is skewed.
Up to six, as percentages. With bars by time held, P95 is the value the tag was at or below for 95% of the time.
